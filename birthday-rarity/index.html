Why some birthdays are more common than others

Births aren't evenly spread across the calendar. In many countries, late-summer and early-autumn months see noticeably more births, a pattern often linked to conception timing around the winter holidays roughly nine months earlier. Meanwhile, certain specific dates are consistently rarer — most notably major holidays like New Year's Day, Christmas, and the days immediately around them.

Part of this is natural seasonal biology, and part of it is logistical: many scheduled inductions and C-sections are deliberately planned around weekdays and away from holidays, which further reduces births on those specific dates.

How this estimate is built

This tool models the general shape of birth-rate seasonality reported in widely cited population data, combined with known dips around major holiday dates, to rank how common or rare each day of the year is relative to all others. It's built for a fun sense of perspective, not as a precise statistical record.
