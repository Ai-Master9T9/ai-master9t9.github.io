Why height varies so much around the world

Average adult height differs noticeably from country to country, shaped by a mix of genetics, nutrition, healthcare access, and childhood living conditions. Nations with strong early-life nutrition and healthcare tend to have taller average populations, while historical or ongoing food insecurity in a region can measurably lower average adult height over generations.

Because of this spread, "average height" is really a range rather than a single fixed number. This tool uses a broad global average and typical variation for men and women, so the estimate reflects a worldwide comparison rather than any single country's standard.

How this estimate is calculated

We use an approximate global average height and standard deviation for each gender, and calculate where your height falls on that distribution using a standard statistical model (a normal distribution curve). This gives a reasonable, fast estimate of your percentile — not a clinical or population-specific measurement.
